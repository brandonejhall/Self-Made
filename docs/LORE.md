# Self Made: Lore Bible

Canon. Exported from the Self Made lore bible doc on 2026-11-09. The lore-writer agent keeps this file and the doc in sync.

## Canon rules

- This document is canon. Agents never contradict it. New lore extends it and gets logged in STATE.md.
- Lore runs in 2 layers. Authored lore lives here: the world timeline, the families, the mysteries. Emergent lore comes from each run: the partners the player cuts out, the deals they sign, the children they raise.
- Authored world events fire on the world clock whatever the player does. The player decides who wins and loses inside them. At Tier 7 the player can delay or trigger 3 of them (listed in the timeline).
- Every lore item ties to a mechanic. If a fact changes no number, cut it.
- Tone: grounded, modern, morally grey. No magic. Frontier tech stays physically plausible.
- Companies, people and governments are fictional. Cities and geography are real.
- The game runs from 2026 to 2086. The player starts at age 21.

## The world: 1952 to 2086

### Before the game

- **1952:** Anselm Voss founds Voss Maritime in Rotterdam with 2 secondhand freighters.
- **1978:** Meridian Holdings, a shell company, buys 40 acres of coastline near each of the 6 starting cities in a single year. Nobody connects the purchases.
- **1997 and 2008:** Two global crashes. Voss Maritime buys distressed ports after both. By 2026 it is Voss Group: ports, commodities and private credit.

### Era 1: The Hangover (2026 to 2032)

High interest rates. AI automation wipes out 30% of entry-level office jobs. Gig work replaces careers. Property prices stall in 4 of the 6 cities.

- **Opportunity:** distressed small businesses, cheap commercial property, side hustles that scale.
- **Threat:** no bank lends to anyone under 30 without collateral.

### Era 2: The Build-Out (2033 to 2040)

The energy transition and the data center boom pull $40 trillion into infrastructure. Lagos and Manila grow fastest. Detroit's empty auto plants become battery factories.

- **2036:** Halden Capital becomes the world's largest private credit fund at $1.1 trillion. Voss Group holds 19% of it.
- **Opportunity:** land near power and water, logistics, construction, energy.

### 2040: The Halden Collapse

Halden Capital fails in 11 days. Credit freezes worldwide. 1 in 4 mid-sized companies on the map goes bankrupt. Whoever holds cash on day 12 buys the world at 20 cents on the dollar.

### Era 3: The Consolidation (2041 to 2055)

Megacorporations absorb the wreckage. Governments start treating billionaires as rival powers. AI compute becomes a strategic resource rationed by treaty.

- **2049: The Strait Closure.** A regional war closes a major shipping strait for 14 months. Fuel triples. Ports and shipping routes outside the war zone become the most valuable assets on Earth. (Tier 7 trigger: a player who owns 3+ ports in the region can shorten it to 6 months.)

### Era 4: The Frontier (2056 to 2070)

- **2058:** The first commercial fusion plant comes online. (Tier 7 trigger: a player who funds a fusion moonshot can pull this to 2054.)
- Orbital manufacturing, longevity medicine and autonomous cities become investable. Elections in 9 of 30 economies depend on billionaire funding.

### Era 5: The Reckoning (2071 to 2086)

A populist wave sweeps 18 of 30 economies. Wealth taxes, forced breakups and asset seizures follow. Dynasties that built goodwill survive. Dynasties that didn't lose 40% to 70% of their holdings. (Tier 7 trigger: a player with public approval above 60 can delay the wave by 8 years.)

## The six starting cities

Each city sets 5 starting values: cost of living, access to credit, passport mobility, disaster risk and informal pressure (bribes, protection demands, connected competitors). Each city also carries one local thread that pays off in Stage 2.

| City | Edge | Handicap | Local thread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kingston | Diaspora networks in Toronto, New York and London | Hurricanes; weak passport | A port expansion tender in 2034 that Voss Group already has a seat on |
| Lagos | The biggest market on the map; fastest growth | Currency swings up to 40% a year; power outages | A strip of Lekki land with 3 competing title claims |
| São Paulo | A huge domestic market | Interest rates near 14%; slow bureaucracy | A bankrupt agribusiness empire sold off in pieces in 2031 |
| Manila | Outsourcing economy; English-speaking workforce | Typhoons; slow courts | Reclaimed land in Manila Bay that a senator wants to rezone |
| Detroit | US passport; cheap property | A declining old economy until 2033 | An abandoned auto plant that the battery boom makes worth $900M |
| Naples | EU market access; cheap historic property | Heavy informal pressure on construction | A port concession whose current holder owes money to the wrong people |

The 1978 Meridian land sits near every starting city. Whichever city the player picks, their family's lost land is part of it.

## Wealth tiers: scrub to world-shaper

Net worth sets the tier. Each tier unlocks new levers and draws a new kind of enemy.

| Tier | Net worth | Title | What unlocks | What comes for you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Under $10K | Scrub | Jobs, side hustles, a first loan | Rent, debt collectors, burnout |
| 1 | $10K to $1M | Hustler | First business, first property, first hire | Bad partners, scams, one bad month |
| 2 | $1M to $10M | Owner | Multiple companies, managers, bank credit | Lawsuits, envy, a partner who wants more |
| 3 | $10M to $100M | Operator | Second city, acquisitions, outside investors | The local press, tax audits, connected competitors |
| 4 | $100M to $1B | Tycoon | Second country, political access, media ownership | National regulators; rival families notice you |
| 5 | $1B to $10B | Magnate | Elections, sports franchises, a security detail | Investigations, kidnapping risk, an unauthorized biography |
| 6 | $10B to $100B | Titan | Deals with heads of state, infrastructure across the map | Foreign governments, coordinated short sellers, antitrust |
| 7 | Over $100B | World-shaper | Moonshots, market-moving posts, world event triggers | Everyone. The Reckoning targets Tier 7 first. |

Every tier also costs time. The player's weekly hours stay fixed at 100. Each tier takes more of those hours unless the player delegates, and every delegated decision runs through a manager who can fail or betray them.

## Great families and rival dynasties

### The Voss family (Rotterdam, since 1952)

The Voss family's rule: never build, buy whatever someone else built. By 2026 Voss Group is worth $310 billion. It owns a stake in whatever industry the player enters.

- **Margarethe Voss (71 in 2026).** Anselm's daughter and the current head of the family. Patient and ruthless. She has never lost a board vote. She dies in 2047, and her will splits the family.
- **Julian Voss (44).** Her son and chosen heir. He runs the private credit arm and engineers the Halden Collapse to buy distressed assets. He becomes the player's main rival in Stage 2.
- **Petra Voss (39).** Her daughter. She left the family business at 25 and runs a climate fund. Publicly she criticizes her family. Privately she is the Patron (see Mysteries).
- **The family secret:** Meridian Holdings, the 1978 land buyer, belongs to Voss.

### Okafor-Bello Group (Lagos)

Cement, telecoms and ports across West Africa. Chief Adaeze Okafor-Bello (63) built it from one cement depot in 1989. She respects anyone who started with nothing. She is the most valuable ally on the map, and she cuts off anyone who embarrasses her in public.

### The Castellano family (São Paulo)

Media and agribusiness. The family has picked 3 of the last 5 presidents of their fictional national government. They offer coverage to friends and destroy enemies on air. Their heir, Rodrigo Castellano (29), starts the game as broke as the player because his father cut him off. He can become the player's first partner in any city.

### The Tan family (Singapore)

Quiet money. The family runs a sovereign-adjacent fund worth $600 billion. They never lead a deal and never lose one. They first contact the player at Tier 4. Their capital always comes with a seat on the board.

### New money: Rafe Kessler

Kessler (34 in 2026) is the world's richest person, with $480 billion from rockets, AI chips and a social network. He is the benchmark the player chases. He overreaches in 2044 with a private city project, loses a fraud trial in 2046 and sells his empire off in pieces. The player can buy any piece they can afford.

## Recurring characters

These characters appear in every run. Their attitude toward the player comes from the player's own actions, tracked as a trust score from -100 to 100.

- **Inés Albuquerque, journalist.** She starts at a 6-person news site in 2026. She wins a major prize in 2041 for her Halden reporting. She covers the player for 40 years, and her final series decides the player's public legacy score.
- **Tobias Wren, fixer.** A lawyer who solves problems and asks no questions. Each favor he does adds a line to his private ledger. He calls in the ledger once per stage, always at the worst moment.
- **Halima Yusuf, banker.** She approves the player's first loan in Stage 1 when nobody else will. She becomes a central bank governor in 2052. A player who repaid her early gets a friend at the top of the financial system.
- **Arturo Vale, regulator.** A career prosecutor who wants to make his name by taking down someone the player's size. He appears at Tier 4 and follows the player up. Clean records frustrate him. Shortcuts feed him.
- **The first partner.** Generated per run. The friend who starts the first business with the player. Cutting them out, buying them out or keeping them close sets up the hardest choice in Stage 2.
- **Margarethe Voss.** She meets the player once per tier from Tier 3 on. Each meeting is an offer to buy everything the player owns, at a price that rises each time.

## Your family

- **Your grandmother** raised you. She dies in year 2 of the game. She leaves a shoebox: old photos, a 1978 land deed and a letter.
- **The letter** is from your grandfather, who died before you were born. It says he signed the family's 40 acres to Meridian Holdings in exchange for "a share that will make this family whole." It names a bank in Zurich and a box number.
- **Your absent parent** left when you were 4. Their name opens doors in one city on the map and closes them in another. They know what the Zurich box holds. They contact you once you reach Tier 3, and they want money.
- **Your sibling** asks for money in Stage 1. In Stage 2 they want a job. In Stage 3 they want a share. How you treat them decides whether they become your strongest ally or the witness against you in Arturo Vale's case.
- **Your children** inherit traits shaped by the hours you spend with them. A neglected child grows up competent and resentful. A raised child grows up loyal and untested. When you die, you play on as one heir, and the others become rivals or partners.

## Master mysteries and reveal schedule

### 1. The Zurich share

- **Stage 1:** The shoebox letter.
- **Stage 2:** You learn Meridian Holdings belongs to Voss. The Zurich bank refuses to open the box without a court order.
- **Stage 3:** The box holds a signed agreement from Anselm Voss granting your grandfather 2% of Voss Maritime. Voss never honored it. By 2050, 2% of Voss Group is worth $6 billion. You can sue, trade the document to Julian for a deal, or use it to blow up the family from inside.

### 2. Who built the Halden Collapse

- **From 2034:** Clues surface in news items, a Halden employee who quits, and odd Voss trades.
- **Before 2040:** A player who connects the clues can short Halden, warn regulators, or quietly hoard cash.
- **After 2040:** Inés Albuquerque's reporting names Julian Voss. Your evidence decides whether the story holds up.

### 3. The Patron

- **Tier 2:** An anonymous investor offers capital through a Cayman shell at a rate no bank would match. It arrives right when you need it most.
- **Tier 4:** The Patron funds you again and asks for one thing: never sell to Voss.
- **Stage 3:** The Patron is Petra Voss. She has been building you as the weapon to break her family. Accepting her final offer makes her your ally. Exposing her makes Julian your ally.

### 4. Rafe Kessler's fall

- **2042 to 2046:** Kessler's private city project starts burning cash. Former engineers leak documents. You can feed the leaks, help him hide them, or wait.
- **2046:** The fraud verdict. Whatever you did decides whether you buy his empire cheap or face your own investigation.

### 5. Your legacy

- **From Tier 5:** An unauthorized biography gets written from your run's actual history.
- **At your death:** Your obituary appears in 3 versions: Inés's, the Castellano network's and your family's. Each one draws on what you actually did.

### Moonshots (Tier 7 only)

Each moonshot costs $20 billion to $80 billion and takes 8 to 15 years. Each has a published chance of success, and every decade of funding shifts those odds.

- **Orbital logistics:** cargo to orbit at $50 per kg. Success at 55%.
- **Commercial fusion:** 4 plants by 2060. Success at 40%. A success pulls the 2058 fusion event earlier.
- **Longevity:** adds 15 years to the player's lifespan and delays succession. Success at 25%.
- **Autonomous city:** a city of 1 million with its own laws, on leased land. Success at 45%. Kessler tried this first and failed.
- **Sovereign AI:** a compute cluster that a government has to license from you. Success at 60%. It triggers an antitrust case within 3 years.

## Lore delivery systems

Lore never arrives as a cutscene. It arrives through the same channels a real person gets information from.

- **Your phone (Stage 1).** Texts from your sibling, your partner and debt collectors. A news feed that shows 3 headlines a week.
- **Your inbox and briefings (Stage 2).** Managers' reports, investor letters, legal notices, a weekly press digest.
- **Your intelligence desk (Stage 3).** Dossiers on rivals, leaked documents, intercepted deals.
- **Physical documents.** The shoebox, board minutes, court filings and the Zurich box. Each one opens a new branch of a mystery.
- **The world press.** Inés's outlet, the Castellano network and business wires all report the same event from 3 angles.
- **Your biography and obituary.** Generated from the run's event log, so the story of your life is the story you actually played.
